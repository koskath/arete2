The image presents a categorization of roles within supply chain management, specifically focusing on intermediaries, hybrid forms, and service providers. Here's how these concepts connect to supply chain analytics:

1. **Intermediaries or Middlemen**:
   - **Commercial Intermediaries (CI)**: Include sales agents and brokers who operate on commodity markets. In supply chain analytics, they play a role in understanding market trends, price fluctuations, and demand forecasting.
   - **Financial Intermediaries (FI)**: Such as confirming houses and brokers, are crucial for financial analysis, risk assessment, and ensuring liquidity within the supply chain.
   - **Transport Intermediaries (TI)**: Such as customs house brokers, who facilitate the movement of goods through regulatory frameworks. Analytics can optimize routes, reduce transit times, and manage compliance.
   - **Logistics Intermediaries (LI)**: Include 4PL and system integrators, crucial for strategic planning and integration of complex logistics networks.

2. **Hybrid Forms**:
   - **Entities** like export merchants and trading companies, along with services like commercial banks and insurance companies, serve dual roles. In supply chain analytics, they provide insights into risk management, transaction safety, and international trade facilitation.

3. **Service Providers**:
   - **Commercial Service Providers (CP)**: Focus on warehousing, customizing, and repackaging. Analytics can streamline inventory management, space utilization, and efficiency measures.
   - **Financial Service Providers (FP)**: Utilize platforms like SWIFT for financial transactions, where analytics help in fraud detection, transaction efficiency, and financial data management.
   - **Transport Service Providers (TP)**: Involving trucking companies, ocean carriers, and airlines, they benefit from analytics through route optimization, capacity planning, and fuel efficiency.
   - **Logistics Service Providers (LP)**: Like 3PLs and public warehouses, rely on data analytics for better supply chain visibility, demand forecasting, and overall efficiency improvement.

4. **International Freight Forwarders and Cargo Consolidators**:
   - Central to the diagram, these entities integrate various supply chain components. They use analytics for optimization of freight costs, consolidation efficiency, and improving the overall speed and reliability of shipments.

Overall, supply chain analytics in this context helps in optimizing operations, reducing costs, managing risks, and improving service levels across various intermediary and service provider roles.
